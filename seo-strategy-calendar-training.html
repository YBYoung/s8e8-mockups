Think first

Before you open a single tab in OQ: what a strategy actually is, and why we start with people instead of a keyword list.

Two ways to plan the same twelve pages. One fills a spreadsheet. The other fills a schedule book.

Keywords are how people phrase a need. Start with the need; use keywords to check you are speaking the patient's language.
Top-down

Four questions, in this order

Answer these in plain sentences before you trust any output. If you can't, the strategy isn't ready, no matter how good the data looks.

1

Who are we trying to reach?

The patient, not the practice. OQ's audience step is told the data "represents the TARGET AUDIENCE (patients/customers), NOT the business itself." Hold yourself to the same rule.

2

What do they need to know before they book?

Fear, cost, recovery, "will it hurt", "is this dentist good with someone like me". Those are pages.

3

What can this practice say that nobody else can?

The doctor's real experience, the technology they actually use, how they handle anxious patients. This is the first E in E-E-A-T, and it is how we escape the sea of sameness.

4

What does success look like for this practice?

More of the cases they want: calls, bookings, the services they want to grow. Traffic alone is not the goal.

I'm 44 and honestly scared of the dentist.
Will it hurt? What will it really cost?
Does this dentist get people like me?
I just want to eat normally again.
Lily Ray, applied

E-E-A-T, with trust at the center

Google calls health topics YMYL ("Your Money or Your Life"): bad information can hurt someone, so Google and the AI engines hold it to their strictest bar. OQ's own SEO principles open with "E-E-A-T is non-negotiable for dental content."

Experience
Expertise
Authority
TRUST

On a dental page, that looks like:

  • A named dentist who wrote or reviewed it, with real credentials and a bio page.
  • First-hand detail: what the doctor actually sees in the chair and how this practice handles it.
  • Only claims we can source. Nothing medical we can't back up.
  • Transparency. In Lily's words, make it clear "who your experts are, who your company is."
A bio box is not a magic ranking factor on its own, a point Lily makes often. The content itself has to earn the trust; the signals just make it visible.

How AI search reads a page

Before we build anything: how Google's AI Mode and other answer engines decide which pages to quote. It's the reason every calendar piece is written the way it is.

AI search, step 1 · Fan-out

One question becomes dozens

Mike King of iPullRank, reading Google's patents: AI Mode quietly turns one patient question into many related searches, then gathers passages for each one. The patient never sees them.

Example searches
How to think

A page can win on a question nobody typed.

  • A practice can be quoted because one section answers one of these hidden follow-ups, even if the page doesn't rank for the original question.
  • In King's words, "ranking for the original query no longer guarantees visibility."
  • So each calendar piece should answer the likely follow-up questions too, not just the headline one.
What OQ automates

When a calendar item moves into production, OQ runs this same kind of fan-out for it, expanding the item's searches into the follow-up questions the draft should cover. There's also a standalone Query Fan-Out tool.

AI search, step 2 · Vector space

Every passage is a point

Search systems turn every query and every passage of text into a vector: a long list of numbers that works like an arrow pointing somewhere in "meaning space". Passages pointing the same way as the question are the relevant ones.

Patient questionPassages from practice pagesClosest passage

Illustration. Real vectors have hundreds or thousands of dimensions; this flattens them to two so you can see the idea.

Pick a patient question
Only the angle counts. The score is the cosine of the angle between two arrows: 1.00 means pointing the same way, around 0 means unrelated. That's why a focused section that answers one question beats a page that touches on everything.

The same math, one level up. OQ's Relevance Analyzer turns every page on the site and a set of target searches into vectors, finds the center of those searches, and measures how closely each page points at it.

AI search, step 3 · Head to head

Two passages in, one gets cited

From Mike King's reading of Google's pairwise ranking patent: the AI compares candidate passages two at a time and keeps the one that better answers the question. The winner gets cited, wherever its page ranks.

A hidden follow-up searchimplant or bridge for a back tooth?
Passage A · a services page
We offer a full range of restorative dentistry services, including dental implants and bridges. Our friendly team is here to help you smile with confidence! Call today to schedule your visit.
VS
CITED
Passage B · a calendar blog article
For a single missing back molar, we usually recommend an implant when the teeth on either side are healthy. A bridge means filing down those two teeth to anchor it; an implant leaves them alone. Dr. Lee walks through both options at your consult.
The AI asks: which of these two better answers the search? B, easily. Example passages
Why B wins
  • Answers the question in its first sentence.
  • Makes sense on its own, pulled out of the page.
  • Specific: names the tradeoff, not just the services.
  • Shows who's talking: a named dentist and real experience.
What we write for

Every calendar piece should be built from sections like B: one question per section, the answer first, specific, and in the dentist's voice. E-E-A-T and AI search are asking for the same thing.

    Save yourself an afternoon

    Gotchas to know

    Things that trip people up today.

    Counts

    Read the "X of Y"

    Duplicate removal can leave you short. Name the missing topics and regenerate, or add them by hand.

    Inputs

    Guidance doesn't protect topics

    Only "Name the topics" slots are pinned. Put must-haves there.

    Time

    Big calendars are slow

    Over 16 pieces can take up to 20 minutes. Leave the tab running.

    Review

    Out for review blocks regenerating

    Cancelling the review to regenerate deletes the client's feedback. Think first.

    Replace

    Undo is short-lived

    Replace with different page can be undone only until you refresh.

    Duplicates

    Old calendars still count

    Rejected and stale calendars count as "planned", so a topic may be blocked by an old plan.

    Email

    No test send for calendars

    A calendar "test" would go to the real client. Preview inside OQ instead.

    Evidence

    "Weak evidence" on your own topics

    A topic you named yourself may list no supporting searches and get a "weak evidence" note from the quality check. That's expected; ignore the note.

    Data

    Missing sources, weaker scores

    Without Search Console, priorities rely on estimated search volume. Say so when you present.
