01 · Summary Draft for discussion

One place for every project, fee and hour at Schorr.

Billing, budgets and timesheets move out of one spreadsheet per project and into one platform for the whole firm. It all ties to one client database, with a forecast months ahead that shows how solvent the firm is at any time.

Prepared for Nathan, Schorr ArchitectsPrepared by Yitzchak YoungNovember 21, 2026Start: second week of December 2026
Proof firstAbout 2 weeks. The platform must reproduce your own billing workbook to the cent. You pay for it only if it passes.
Six stages, built from what you asked forEach one tested by your team on real work and accepted before it counts. You can stop after any accepted stage and keep everything you've paid for.
No leap of faithYour spreadsheet stays the official record for 3 to 6 months while the platform runs alongside it. You switch over when you're satisfied.

This proposal sets out the work, how it is tested, how changes are handled and what each side does. It isn't a contract yet: once we agree the direction and budget, it becomes a written agreement both sides review. The short walkthrough is here.

A proof step, then six stages, built from what you asked for.

Choose a stage to see what it includes, the checks it must pass and what it needs from your team. The checks shown are examples; we agree the exact list for each stage in writing before it starts.

Optional add-on: a task appTask lists for the whole firm, tied to milestones and billing. Offered separately, only if you want it.
After the first versionReading RFQs straight from district websites, which differ site by site and sometimes block automated reading; and an AI recommendation on go or no-go, which works better once a few years of pursuits, won and lost, are recorded. Either can come earlier if your team wants; the demo shows how they could look.

Stage 5 option

If you move to QuickBooks Online

It finishes tying everything together, as you asked. Sage 50 works with a monthly file. QuickBooks Online would make the hand-off automatic, and switching is your Controller's call, never a requirement.

What changes

Invoices

Go across to QuickBooks as soon as they're issued in the platform.

Payments

Payments your Controller records in QuickBooks come back automatically, checked at least once a day, so collections and the forecast stay within a day of your books. No monthly file, and nobody re-typing.

New clients and bills

New clients and engineer bills go across by themselves.

Other programs

The QuickBooks-only programs you mentioned could become an option, if they work with QuickBooks Online.

What it takes

Only after it's proven.

We first test the connection against a practice QuickBooks company that Intuit (the company that makes QuickBooks) provides for testing, then run it side by side with your current books for at least one month end. You switch only once it matches, the same way as the overlap period.

What it takes

Your Controller's decision; moving your accounting history across, best timed to the start of a fiscal year; and Intuit's approval of the connection before it can use live data.

Intuit's approval

The connection is private to your firm and never listed in Intuit's app store, so it only needs Intuit's compliance questionnaire instead of a full app store review. Intuit says results usually show within minutes. If Intuit asks follow-up questions, it sets no deadline for its answer, so we'd start early, while testing on the practice company.

Intuit's fees: none expected.

Intuit's free plan doesn't count invoices the platform creates one at a time. Reading invoices and payments back comes with 500,000 free look-ups a month, far more than one firm needs.

One step for your team

Someone at the firm with QuickBooks administrator access approves the connection once. They approve it again if it's ever disconnected or what it can reach changes, and at least every five years. QuickBooks shows a notice 30 days ahead and sends an email 7 days ahead. The Intuit account used to build the connection is in the firm's name, like every other account.

What stays the same: your books stay in your accounting software, Sage or QuickBooks. The platform never holds the ledger, on purpose, because that's the safer setup for your firm.

Accounting software already does that job well.

Tax forms, payroll, bank feeds and year-end close are built, tested and updated by the vendor every year as rules change. Rebuilding them would add cost and risk and give you nothing new.

Your Controller keeps working the way they do now.

Year-end, taxes and any outside accountant's review still come from the system they already know and trust.

Your most sensitive data stays in one place.

Bank details, payroll and tax records never sit on the platform, so there's less to protect.

Your books never depend on the platform.

If it's paused, changed or replaced, your accounting carries on untouched.

05 · How a stage is accepted

"Done" means it passed the checks we agreed, on your real work.

We agree the checks first.

Before a stage starts, we write down a short list of what it must do, using real examples from your work.

We tell you in writing when it's ready.

Your team then has 10 business days to try it on real work. We send a reminder before the time is up.

If something stops you working (a blocking problem), we fix that first,

and the 10 days pause until it's fixed. Smaller problems go on a list and get fixed without holding things up.

Up to three rounds of fixes

are included in each stage.

The stage is accepted

when you say so in writing, or if the 10 days end with no reply after our reminder.

We start the next stage while you test this one,

so no time is lost waiting.

06 · The overlap period

Your spreadsheet stays the official record for 3 to 6 months.

Invoices keep coming from your workbook as they do today while the platform runs alongside it. At each month end, a comparison shows workbook against platform, project by project and line by line, with every difference explained. If something doesn't match, nobody is billed wrongly; we find out why and fix it.

No double entry.

You keep your workbook as usual. Each month we bring the same percent-complete figures in from it, so nobody types them twice, and the platform works out its own bills.

You switch over when you choose,

in writing, for example after three month ends in a row match to the cent.

The same idea applies to each stage

budgets against your budget tab, timesheets against however hours are tracked today.

SwapLogged, not chargedTrading one idea for another of the same size within the stage. For example, a different summary view instead of one already planned.
AdditionPriced in writingGenuinely new work. You get a written price and timing within 3 business days. It starts only once you approve it by email.

Chat messages and calls are for discussion; approvals are by email so there is a record. Small quality-of-life improvements after the platform is in use can come from the monthly hours in the "Care and improve" plan.

11 · Security and your data

Security is checked at every step. An outside test is your choice.

Every changeAutomatic tests look for exposed passwords, unsafe code, and outside code with known security flaws. Another test opens every screen as every type of user, to confirm each person sees only what they should. If any test fails, the change can't go live.
Every releaseEach release is a batch of changes we put live. It gets an AI security review of everything that changed, a check of the website's security settings, and a dated record of what was released. We review changes to sign-in, access, exports or files right away, without waiting for a release.
Each stageA full security review of the stage before it's handed to you, with every fix recorded.
Optional, yearlyAn independent security firm tries to break in before your data goes live, then once a year. We recommend it; it's your call. You choose the firm and pay them directly.

Signing in and who sees what

Two-factor authentication for everyone

Staff sign in with their own Microsoft 365 accounts, and each sign-in needs a second step, such as a code or an approval in the Microsoft Authenticator app, on top of the password. The platform refuses a sign-in without it. If it isn't already on for everyone, we set it up with your Microsoft 365 administrator. No new passwords.

Who sees what

Cost, rate and profit figures only reach people whose access level allows it, on screen, in exports and in search.

When someone leaves

Their access is switched off in one step inside the platform, as well as in Microsoft. Once a quarter, your Controller reviews and approves who can see what.

The accounts that run it

Every account used to run the platform, including ours and your hosting and backup accounts, requires two-factor authentication too.

Activity record

Sign-ins, exports and views of cost figures. Any time we open your live data, the platform records it and we must give a reason. That record is copied every night to storage we can't change.

Where your data lives

Where it lives

Your records are on Render's servers in Ohio, in an account in your firm's name, encrypted (scrambled so only the platform can read it) both while it travels over the internet and while it's stored. Attached files (agreements, amendments, invoices, receipts) are kept in a private, encrypted storage account at a second company, Backblaze. Nightly database backups go there too, encrypted before they leave so Backblaze can't read them, and locked so they can't be deleted. With the backups at a second company, one outage won't take out both. Only the services the platform needs to run ever hold any of it, each for one job, and nothing is sold or shared for any other purpose. You'll get the full list, and we won't add a new service without telling you first.

A test copy (staging)

A separate copy of the platform in your hosting account, holding a copy of your data with names, contact details and rates scrambled. Every change is checked there first, and new work can be shown to you there, without touching your live records.

Never in the platform

Your ledger, payroll, taxes, payments, bank details and Social Security numbers. They stay in your accounting software.

Your files during the build

We keep the fee tables and invoices you share for the proof step encrypted on our computer and use them only for this project. Once the proof passes, we delete them, along with the AI coding assistant's working records. The assistant's provider keeps its own copy of those records for up to 30 days, then deletes them.

If something goes wrong

If it ever goes down

Usually only a few minutes of work could be lost. If the hosting company itself failed, we would restore from the last nightly backup, about a day back, and you'd get an alert if a backup were ever missed. We aim to have you back up within one business day, and we prove it with a timed practice restore before your data goes live, and again every year.

Security fixes

Urgent ones go out within 72 hours of a fix being available, without waiting for the next release.

If there's ever a security problem

There's a written plan for it, and you're told about any suspected breach within a time set in the agreement.

At the end

Your data is yours. If we ever stop working together, you get all of it back, and every copy we hold is deleted within 30 days, with a written list of what was deleted.

The platform isn't built yet, so these are planned. Before your real data goes in, you'll get this list back with each item confirmed and dated.

12 · Warranty and support

Software that keeps improving after you start using it.

Warranty: each stage is covered until it has been used for two month-end closes, or until the overlap period ends, whichever is later. Anything that fails the agreed checks is fixed at no cost. After that, support continues on a monthly plan; monthly amounts and included hours are agreed together.

CareFixes for anything broken; security updates; we watch the backups, with a practice restore, and the alerts that warn us if the platform goes down or shows errors; and the platform's connection to Microsoft sign-in is renewed before it expires, with reminders to both sides, so sign-in never stops.
Care and improveEverything in Care, plus a set number of hours each month for improvements from your change list and the small quality-of-life fixes that make daily use smoother.
When something's wrong

You tell us in one place, with a screenshot if you have one. We recreate the problem and write an automatic test that fails because of it. We fix it until that test and every other test pass, try the fix on the test copy, and only then put it live. You get a short note saying what changed.

Proposed response times

(to agree): the same business day when billing or sign-in is blocked; within two business days for everything else.

13 · Assumptions and exclusions

What this proposal assumes, and what it leaves out.

Assumes

Accounting software

Your accounting software is Sage 50. Your Controller will confirm.

Sign-in

Your staff sign in with Microsoft 365, and someone, in-house or your IT firm, can let the platform use Microsoft sign-in.

Billing method

The other project managers bill the way your workbook does: the same seven milestones, billed by percent complete. If some work differently, we'll find out in stage 2 and agree how to handle it.

Firm size

About 36 staff, with four or five access levels, for example principals, project managers, the Controller and everyone else.

RFQ alerts

RFQ alerts can be sent to one dedicated email inbox that the platform is allowed to read.

Not included

Your accounting itself

The ledger, payroll, taxes and payments stay in your accounting software; section 3 explains why.

Project documents

Drawings, specifications and other project documents stay where you keep them today; the platform only keeps the agreements, amendments, invoices and receipts that billing relies on.

A phone app

The platform works in the web browser on any phone, tablet or computer.

Older projects

The platform starts from what's in your current workbook; older history can be added later as a separately priced piece.

If an assumption is wrong: we'll tell you as soon as we find out, explain what it changes, and agree any change in writing before going on, the same way as any other addition.

14 · Pausing or ending the work

You can stop after any accepted stage and keep everything you've paid for.

Stopping

After any accepted stage, with written notice. You pay for accepted stages, and the 25% already invoiced for the next stage covers work started on it.

What you keep

The code for every paid stage, the accounts (already yours), your data, and a handover: how it's set up, how to restore it, and where everything lives.

Continuity

You get a document with the recovery instructions, the key that unlocks the encrypted backups, and where the code lives. Keep it somewhere safe at the firm, so you can carry on if we are ever unavailable. Once a year, someone other than us uses it to practice a restore.

To finalize in the written agreement

A limit on liability tied to the fees paid, no responsibility for indirect losses (for example, lost business), and your Controller reviewing each invoice before it's sent.

15 · Who's building it

Thirteen months building and running an agency's operating system.

Since October 2025, Yitzchak has designed and run the in-house platform his search marketing (SEO) agency's team relies on every day. It began as content-writing tools. Since March 2026, it has grown to cover the SEO department's whole workflow: audience research, market analysis, content calendars, content production, publishing to clients' Webflow sites, performance reporting and more. Clients use it too, through secure links where they approve content calendars, review drafts and open their reports.

Alongside the core workflow, the platform tracks how often clients' businesses appear in AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews. It scans local Google Maps rankings, checks client websites page by page, and scores, for leadership, how far behind each area of the team's work is. It connects two ways with Asana and reads from Google Analytics, Search Console, Business Profiles and the agency's customer database (CRM). It also keeps a permanent archive of client analytics that Google stops providing after 14 to 18 months.

It has replaced five paid marketing-data subscriptions (Peec AI, SparkToro, Screaming Frog, Semrush and Local Falcon) at a fraction of their cost, and it fits how the agency uniquely works. Most requested changes go live within a day or two. Live walkthroughs are available on request.

How it's built. Yitzchak leads the software's design: what gets built, how it behaves, and what counts as done. AI coding assistants write the code under his direction, inside a disciplined process. More than 17,000 automated tests guard the code. Irreplaceable data such as analytics history and AI answer records is only ever added to, never overwritten or deleted. Long-running jobs recover cleanly if a server restarts; the in-depth AI search visibility analysis, for example, picks up where it left off. Your project goes a step further: every change must pass automatic accuracy tests, access tests for every type of user and security scans before it can go live, and each stage is accepted only after it passes the checks agreed with you.

16 · Terms used

Plain meanings for the terms in this proposal.

We (the builder)

Yitzchak Young, who builds and supports the platform.

Proof step

A short first step that proves the platform reproduces your own workbook to the cent before anything else is built.

Stage

A piece of the platform your team uses for real, tested and accepted on its own, before the next one counts.

Checks

The short written list of what a stage must do, agreed before it starts. Automatic tests are separate: they run on every change.

Acceptance

Your written confirmation that a stage passed its agreed checks, or no reply after our reminder once the 10-business-day testing window closes.

Blocking problem

A problem that stops your team doing their work in the stage. We fix it first, and your 10 testing days pause until it's fixed.

Overlap period

The 3 to 6 months when your spreadsheet stays the official record and the platform runs alongside it for comparison.

Change list

The shared log of every request, sorted into clarifications, swaps and additions.

Access level

What a person is allowed to see and do in the platform, for example whether cost and rate figures reach them.

Two-factor authentication

A second step at sign-in, such as a code or an app approval on your phone, on top of the password, so a stolen password alone can't get in.

Staging (test copy)

A separate copy of the platform, holding a copy of your data with names, contact details and rates scrambled, where every change is checked before it goes live. It never touches your live records.

Release

A batch of changes we put live at one time, with a dated record of what changed.

Repository

The online home of the platform's code and its full history, in the firm's name.

Outside security test

An independent firm, hired with written permission, tries to break in and gives you a dated letter of what it found and that it was fixed.
