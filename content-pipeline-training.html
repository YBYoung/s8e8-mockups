Two voices we keep in the room

Mike King and Google, together

They sound like they disagree. They don't, once you read both carefully.

Mike King · How AI Mode Works

Pages are retrieved a passage at a time

AI search breaks a question into many smaller searches, pulls the best passages for each, and builds an answer from them. A section that is complete on its own can be quoted on its own.

For us: every section of every page should make sense to a reader who landed on it directly. That is also how the writer in OQ is instructed.

Google · Optimizing for generative AI features

No special format is required

Google's own guide lists "content chunking" and "rewriting for AI" as myths, and says non-commodity, first-hand content is the most influential long-term factor.

For us: self-contained sections are a writing discipline that helps readers and retrieval. Nobody is chopping pages into fragments for a robot.

The rule we actually follow: write the page a nervous patient would want to read, in sections that each answer one question completely. Both voices agree on that.
01

Three kinds of page

A service page, a blog article and a pillar page are built differently on purpose. Here is the shape of each and why.

Page type 1 of 3

The service page

For a patient who has mostly decided and is choosing where to go. Every section answers a decision-stage question, not a curiosity.

Dental Implants in Springfield, IL | Example Dental

1,250 words and up · title = [Service] in [City], [State] | [Client]
  1. 1Opening with feeling, key details and a call to action. Then a Quick Takeaways list, 5 to 7 bullets.
  2. 2Three to five body sections on the decisions: am I a candidate, what happens, how long, recovery, cost.
  3. 3Pricing and insurance, from the practice's facts only. Never an invented carrier.
  4. 4What makes this practice different, in the practice's voice.
  5. 5FAQ, 4 to 6 questions. Required. QA fails the piece without it.
  6. 6Closing call to action with the real phone number. At most three CTAs on the whole page.
What makes it different: the writer is told, in capitals, that this is a service page and not a blog. No educational explainers. Umbrella services get one "Services We Provide" list that links to each sub-service page.
How to think

Read the draft as the patient who has three tabs open and is picking a practice. Does every section help them pick? If a section teaches instead of helps, it belongs in a blog.

What OQ automates
  • Enforces the opening, Quick Takeaways and FAQ in the outline review.
  • Caps body sections at 250 words and the whole page under 2,000.
  • Keeps phone numbers and CTAs out of the middle sections, except one.
  • Flags a missing FAQ, a missing closing CTA or a fourth CTA.
Page types 2 and 3 of 3

The blog article and the pillar page

One has an angle. The other has a map.

Blog article

Implant or bridge for a missing back tooth?

1,600 words and up · one question, one angle
  1. 1A relatable opening, then a direct answer in 2 or 3 sentences, like a journalist's first paragraph. The writer is told this "targets retrieval".
  2. 2Four to six sections that build a story, not a list of facts. "Every blog article has an angle."
  3. 3Links up to the parent service page, once or twice, where it fits.
  4. 4No "Key Takeaways", no "In Summary", no recap section. No FAQ required.
Why: a blog earns a visit from someone still deciding. The answer up top is what AI search quotes; the story is what keeps a human reading to the service page link.
Pillar page

The complete guide to replacing a missing tooth

2,800 words and up · breadth here, depth in the blogs
  1. 1Overview plus a 2 to 4 sentence direct answer.
  2. 2Six to nine major sections, organized as a hub. Each one self-contained: "a reader may land on any section directly".
  3. 3Links out to the blog articles and service pages that go deeper on each section.
  4. 4Closing next steps. No textbook tone, no recap section.
Why: the pillar is the page a practice can own a topic with. It covers the whole decision; the spokes cover each turn in it.
How they fit

Hub and spoke

The pillar covers breadth. Service pages convert. Blogs go deep on one question each and point back up. Links are what make this a shape instead of a pile.

How to think

When you review a blog draft, find the one service page it should send readers to. If you cannot name it, the blog has no job.

What OQ automates
  • Tells the writer: blogs link to the parent service page, pillars link out to supporting content.
  • It does not check that those links exist. That is your read, and the Internal Links tool in chapter 4.

02

Keyword clusters

Where a page's topic comes from, what travels with it into the draft, and how the site is arranged around it.

From searches to a page

A cluster is a group of real searches

Not a keyword from a tool. The searches the practice already shows up for in Search Console, grouped by meaning, scored, and attached to one calendar item.

Searches"dental implants springfield il", "implant cost without insurance", "am i too old for implants", "implant vs bridge back tooth" and 30 more, straight from Search Console
ClusterDental implants: 8 to 15 named clusters per site, each with its searches, its pages and a trend (growing, stable, decaying)
RatingDemand vs how well the site already covers it, plus forum complaints. HIGH opportunity means people ask and the site does not answer
ItemOne calendar item: the cluster name, its real supporting searches (never invented), the forum quotes, the angle, the funnel stage and a word target
What reaches the writer
  • The angle (framing notes), the topic's rating and a short synthesis.
  • Up to 8 of the supporting searches, on one line, in the outline step only.
  • The forum quotes that pass relevance, 3 per section at most.
  • Practice facts, doctors, locations, voice, the site's pages.

The section writer never sees the searches. It writes to the outline. Nothing scores "did we answer every search" after the draft. That check is yours.

How to think

Open the Context tab on any piece and read the supporting searches. Then read the draft. Every search should have a sentence that answers it. If one does not, that is your first edit.

Why clusters matter more now

One question becomes many

AI search runs several related searches behind one question (Mike King calls it fan-out, and Google now uses the same word). A page that answers the whole cluster gets quoted for more of them.

The patient typesis a dental implant worth it?
implant vs bridge for a back toothhow long do implants lastimplant cost without insuranceam I too old for an implantimplant dentist near Springfield

Five of these are sections on the Dental Implants page. One is the blog. The cluster told the calendar which was which.

OQ's recommendations panel does this on purpose

For a priority topic it expands the question into 20 follow-ups, scores every existing page against each one by meaning (covered at 0.69 and up, thin at 0.64), and groups the gaps into 4 to 8 proposed pages, each listing the questions it would answer.

That is the calendar stage. It is the same idea as the Relevance Analyzer in Training #1, pointed at a single topic.

Being cited matters more than being clicked. Mike King's line. A self-contained section that answers one follow-up completely is the unit that gets cited.
Site architecture

Group what belongs together

Google's own SEO Starter Guide: group topically similar pages in directories. It is the only Google-authored advice on site structure, and it is enough.

A dental site that reads as a shape
/services/dental-implants, dental-bridges, dentures, bone-grafting, sedation-dentistry
/guides/replacing-a-missing-tooth (the pillar)
/blog/implant-or-bridge-back-tooth, implant-cost-itemized, how-long-bridges-last
/about/dr-priya-patel, dr-marcus-nguyen, the office

The Internal Links tool groups the crawl by exactly these first folders, so a tidy site makes linking easier too.

How to think

A new piece should have an obvious folder. If it does not, it is either the wrong page type or a topic the site is not ready for.

What OQ automates
  • The slug on push to Webflow, from the title.
  • Breadcrumb schema (Home, Services, Dental Implants) on every piece.
  • Which Webflow collection the piece lands in.

The folders themselves are the client's site. We work inside them; we do not rebuild them from a content piece.

03

How a draft gets written

What happens in the minute after you click Generate, what the writer is told, and what the quality checks catch.

Behind Generate

Five passes, not one

No single prompt writes a page. The work is split so each pass can be strict about one thing.

1

Context

The calendar item is frozen into a snapshot: angle, searches, quotes, practice facts, voice, the site's pages.

2

Outline

Section headings planned to the page type's rules. The only pass that sees the supporting searches.

3

Outline review

A second model checks the outline against the required elements and sends it back if something is missing.

4

Sections

Each section written on its own, in the client's voice if we have samples, with the facts it is allowed to use.

5

Checks and auto-fix

Em dashes stripped, any H1 demoted, invented links removed, then the QA checks. Up to two rewrite rounds for the fixable ones.

!

Not generated here

Meta description (calendar step or the Generate button in the meta bar), schema, internal links beyond a few, images. Each is its own click.

Then: Draft Ready

Asana gets "Article Draft Generated! Pending Internal SEO QA." and tags the SEO only. The MS is not tagged on purpose: nothing has happened for the client yet.

The writer's standing orders

What every section is told

These rules apply to every page type. When a draft breaks one, it is usually because a fact was missing, not because the writer ignored the rule.

Rules in the prompt
NeverOur implants are top-rated — ask about financing.
InsteadOur implants come with a written estimate. Ask about financing.
  • No H1 in the body. The page title is the only H1; any other is demoted to an H2 on save.
  • Every section stands alone. No "that said" openers; a reader may land anywhere.
  • Three or more parallel items go in a list. Prose hides them from retrieval.
  • Never invent insurers, doctors, prices, awards, neighborhoods or statistics. Leave a bracketed placeholder instead.
  • Phone numbers only as supplied, as a tap-to-call link.
  • The doctor's casual name once, in the opening. Not in every section.
Placeholder

"Most dental plans treat implants as a major service. [add accepted insurance carriers] We will check your benefits before your consult."

A placeholder is the writer doing the right thing. Your job is to fill it from the Context tab's practice facts, or ask the MS to get the fact from the practice.

How to think

Internal Review is a read, not a button. The piece moves to Internal Review on your first save after generation. Nothing automated happens in that status; it is the one place a human reads the whole page before a client does.

The QA tab

What the checks catch

Deterministic checks, run at generation and whenever you click Re-run QA. Critical fails the piece; a warning is a nudge.

    04

    Internal links

    Where link targets come from, how anchors get chosen, and which rules the tool enforces for you.

    The Internal Links button

    Links come from the crawl

    The writer adds one or two per section while drafting, only to pages whose name matches the heading. The real pass is the button in the toolbar, after you save.

    CrawlThe client's accepted site crawl: sitemap plus Webflow pages. No crawl, no links: "Accept a crawl before generating internal links."
    SectionsYou tick which parts of the site count (Services, About, Blog). The homepage is never a target.
    RankPages ranked by word overlap with the piece; the top 60 go to the AI.
    AnchorsChosen from words already in the text, word for word. One anchor per target, no repeats, scored 0 to 100.
    ChecksAnchor really present, no overlap with an existing link, target in the crawl, no duplicates, target still live (404s dropped). Then Apply N changes.
    Enforced by the tool
    • Never the homepage, never the page itself.
    • No duplicate targets, no duplicate anchors.
    • Only pages that exist in the crawl.
    • About one link per 150 words: at least 5, at most 12.
    • Fix links tab: repeated targets, dead links, links to the wrong domain.
    Only in the prompt, so yours to check

    "Blogs link to the parent service page." "Pillars link out to their spokes." The writer is told; nothing verifies. After Apply, read the list: is the one link that matters in it?

    05

    Client review

    The send, what the client sees, the three rules, the clock, and how we address what comes back.

    Send for Review

    Three steps, no deadline to set

    The button is on every piece in Draft Ready or Internal Review. The modal walks you through who, which email, and a preview.

    1 · Recipients

    One reviewer

    Saved recipients as checkboxes, an "add new" form, a CC field. The app lets you pick several. We pick one (next slide).

    2 · Template

    Which email, and who is copied

    Service page review or blog review. The account manager and the SEO are BCC'd, so the team always sees exactly what the client got.

    3 · Edit and preview

    Read it like the client

    Edit the copy, check the subject, Send Test to Me. The deadline in the email comes from the strategy settings: 14 days unless changed.

    What happens on Send
    • Status: Client Review. The editor locks.
    • One review link per piece, reused for every round, valid 30 days from creation.
    • Asana: "Send to Client For Approval" done, phase Awaiting Approval, a comment tagging the SEO and the MS.
    • Schedule Send does the same later; Auto-Approve skips the client entirely (and says so in Asana).
    How to think

    Before you click Send, open the Context tab one last time. A client should never be the one to find a placeholder, a wrong phone number or a doctor who left the practice.

    The review page

    What the client sees

    A name gate, the page, a comment sidebar, and two decisions. There is no "submit feedback" button; comments and suggested edits save the moment they are made.

    Three things they can do
    CommentOn a highlighted passage, or general from the sidebar. Each comment emails the team at once and posts to Asana tagging the SEO and MS.
    Suggest editHighlight, type a replacement. Shown as strike-through and insert. Suggestions do not notify one by one; they travel with the revision request.
    DecideApprove Content (optional final note) or Request Revision (a confirm dialog explains the one-round rule). The first decision closes the round.
    After they decide

    Approved: a full-page "Content Approved. Thank you for your review. The team will proceed with publishing." Piece status Final.

    Revision requested: they stay on the page with a banner: "sent a revision request... You will get a fresh link when the new version is ready." Piece status Revision Requested, and your editor unlocks.

    Later, same link: decisions are closed, but comments still work as late feedback. Late feedback emails the team and posts to Asana marked "review not reopened". It never reopens the round.

    The three rules

    Two the app enforces, one we do

    Say them to clients exactly this way. The labels matter because a client will test them.

    We cannot edit while the client is reviewing.The API refuses content saves and version restores in Client Review; the editor goes read-only; the suggestion controls say "Available once the client submits the piece for revision". It unlocks by itself when they approve or request a revision. Admins can recall a piece (status dropdown, Client Review to Internal Review) if a review must be pulled.
    Enforced by OQ
    The client gets one revision.A second Request Revision is refused: "Maximum revisions (1) already used." Suggested edits are refused after round one. The final round shows "This is your final review round" with Approve as the only decision. If the final-round deadline passes, the piece auto-approves and any comments become optimization notes.
    Enforced by OQ
    One person from the practice reviews.The app lets you send to several people plus CC, and they all get the same link. Anyone can comment, and the first person to click Approve or Request Revision closes the round for everyone. That is exactly why we ask each practice to name one reviewer: one voice, one decision, no contradictions.
    Our rule, not enforced
    MS, this one is yours: when a second doctor asks for the link, the answer is "send your notes to Dr. Patel, she is the reviewer on this page". Not "sure, it works for anyone".
    The clock

    Fourteen days, then three more

    A sweep runs every five minutes. Nothing waits forever, and nothing is lost quietly.

    Day 0

    Sent

    Deadline set to today plus 14 days (the strategy's setting).

    Day 7

    Reminder

    A nudge a week before the deadline, if the link was never opened. (Built, awaiting release.)

    Day 14

    Deadline

    No feedback at all: auto-approved, status Final. Unsubmitted comments: 3 grace days and a nudge email.

    Day 17

    Grace ends

    Their comments become a revision request automatically. Round one is used.

    Round 2

    Final round

    Deadline passes again: auto-approved. Comments go to the team as optimization notes.

    What OQ automates
    • Every deadline outcome above, with an Asana comment that says which one happened and why.
    • Extend Review Period: resets the date and emails the client the new one.
    • Put Back in Review: for a piece that auto-approved unseen; a fresh email, a reset deadline, the revision count back to zero.
    MS: when to step in

    Day 10 with no visit is a phone call, not an email. An auto-approval the practice never saw is a trust problem later, even though it is "allowed".

    Revision Requested

    Addressing the feedback

    The Client Feedback tab lists every comment and suggestion. Work it top to bottom, then choose how the piece goes back.

    SuggestAccept applies the client's wording to the page and saves a version. Dismiss leaves it. Either way the client sees a resolved note ("Accepted suggested edit" or "Dismissed"). An optional decision note stays internal.
    CommentFix the text, then Reply so the client sees what changed, then Resolve. A reply the client can read is the difference between "handled" and "ignored".
    FactsA comment that asks for a claim (an insurer, a credential) is not a fact source. Check the practice facts first; if the fact is missing, the MS gets it from the practice.
    Two ways back
    • Send Back Revision: the same send modal with a "revision resubmitted" email. The client gets the final round.
    • Feedback Applied, Move Forward: straight to Final, no second look. Optional courtesy email that needs no action. For small fixes the client does not need to re-read.

    Both post to Asana and tag the SEO and MS. The MS decides which one fits the relationship.

    How to think

    If the client would feel the need to check your work, send it back. If they would be annoyed to be asked again, move forward and tell them what you did.

    06

    Images, schema, publish

    The three steps after approval, in the order they have to happen, and why.

    Step 1 after approval

    Images: the practice's own photos

    Nothing is generated. Photos come from the client's Google Drive folder, picked by an AI that looks at the pictures, with alt text written for you and editable.

    SourceThe strategy's connected Drive folder. Without it: "No Google Drive folder connected." Drag-and-drop and a pasted URL also work.
    AI SelectionScans every subfolder, looks at the photos, picks one featured image and up to three inline, one per section at most, never the same photo twice.
    Alt textUnder 125 characters, describes what the photo shows, the practice or city only when natural, no keyword stuffing, no em dashes. A regenerate button per image.
    HostingUploaded with the original file name cleaned up, sized for the featured slot and the body, imported by Webflow on push. Nothing is renamed "for SEO".

    Google's image guidance, in one line: descriptive alt text and file names, modern formats, lazy-load what is below the fold. The tool does the first and third; the client's file names do the second.

    Our rule, not enforced

    Why we wait until approval

    The editor allows photos from Draft Ready onward. We add them after the client approves, because the client is reviewing words, and photos placed under headings get moved or dropped when a revision changes the sections. Approval first, photos second.

    What OQ automates
    • The matching, the alt text, the hosting, the Webflow import.
    • Nothing in Asana. There is no images subtask.
    • Regenerating a piece drops its inline images but keeps the featured one.
    Step 2 after approval

    Schema: built from facts, last of all

    Structured data is a block of labeled facts search engines read alongside the page. OQ assembles it from the practice's data and the page's own headings. The AI writes only the one-line description.

    WebSiteThe practice's site.
    DentistName, address, phone, hours, and the doctors as people. Built once per practice; the location type (storefront, service area, both) decides whether an address shows.schema.org puts Dentist under both LocalBusiness and MedicalBusiness. Google says: use the most specific subtype. So we do.
    WebPageThis page, with the featured image as its main image.
    BreadcrumbListHome, Services, Dental Implants.
    Service For service pages. Blogs and pillars get BlogPosting instead.
    FAQPageOnly when the page has a real FAQ section; the questions are copied word for word.FAQ rich results stopped showing in Google in May 2026. We keep the block because it is accurate and harmless, not because it earns a rich result.
    Not emittedMedicalProcedure, Organization, Article, Speakable. None has a Google rich result for a practice page.
    What OQ automates
    • Generated only when you click the schema icon, then "Generate Schema".
    • A validator shows "N sections checked, X missing fields, Y recommended" against Google's required and recommended fields.
    • Pushed to Webflow as raw JSON-LD in a text field the site template injects.
    The one thing to remember

    Any change to the body clears the stored schema. So schema is the last click before Webflow, after images, after the typo you just noticed. If you edit again, regenerate.

    Step 3 after approval

    Publish: draft first, live second, label by hand

    Three clicks in two places, and one of them is not in OQ.

    OQ · piece

    Push to Webflow

    Only on a Final piece with Webflow connected. Picks the collection, fills 12 fields (name, slug, body, summary, meta, featured image and alt, schema...). Creates a draft item.

    Asana

    Subtask done

    "Publish Content on Webflow" is marked complete. The phase does not change.

    OQ · list, Webflow view

    Publish Live

    Flips the Webflow item live. The page is on the client's site. The piece status is still Final.

    OQ · admin

    Set the label

    Webflow Draft and Published are labels an admin sets from the status dropdown. No email, no Asana, no Webflow call.

    Asana · by hand

    Phase: Published

    The app never sets this. Someone on the team moves it, and that move counts on the dashboard.

    Why the draft step exists: the client's Webflow is their live site. A draft lets you open it in Webflow, see it in their template, and catch a layout problem before any patient does. Locally, all Webflow writes are blocked by design; only production can publish.

    07

    Optimization over time

    Pages that already exist get a different door. Knowing which door is half the job.

    Existing pages only

    The calendar makes new pages. Optimization fixes old ones.

    Training #1's rule, seen from the other side. Optimization works on items that already exist in the client's Webflow.

    InventorySync Webflow pages; group by page type or by topic cluster; accept the crawl.
    ImportPulls the live body, SEO fields and featured image into an editable copy, keeps the original, creates an Asana task.
    OptimizeOne holistic rewrite with the practice facts and the right structure for the page type, then proofread and fact checks. Refused if it would cut the page below half its length.
    FinishTitle and meta, schema, images, Internal Linking: the same buttons as production.
    PushBack to the same Webflow item as an unpublished draft. Someone publishes in Webflow; the Asana phase moving to Published marks the page done.
    Where optimization work comes from
    • "Suggested updates" the calendar generator writes when it sees a thin existing page.
    • Final-round client comments after an auto-approval: delivered as "on-page optimization candidates".
    • The Optimize button on a new draft runs the same engine; the team uses it before Internal Review when a draft reads flat.
    How to think

    A thin Veneers page is never a calendar item. A new question nobody on the site answers is never an optimization. If you are unsure, check the crawl: does the page exist?

    Gotchas to know

    Things that trip people up today.

    Schema

    Edits clear it

    Fix a typo after generating schema and the schema is gone. Regenerate, then push.

    Status

    Final means live, or not

    Pushing and publishing never change the piece status. Check the Webflow view, not the chip.

    Links

    No crawl, no links

    "Accept a crawl before generating internal links." The crawl lives on the Optimization page.

    Review

    The link is shared

    Several people can open it and the first decision wins. Name one reviewer before you send.

    Send

    Re-send resets the clock

    Re-Send for Review gives a fresh 14 days. Extend Review Period is the gentler tool.

    Manual

    A manual Client Review has no link

    The team sometimes sets Client Review by hand for reviews handled outside the app. No token, no email. That is deliberate.

    Tags

    Draft generated tags the SEO only

    The MS is tagged from "sent for review" onward. Not a bug.

    Images

    Regenerate drops inline photos

    The featured image survives a regenerate; the inline ones do not.

    Local

    Local never publishes

    On a developer's machine every Webflow write is blocked. Only production can touch a client site.
