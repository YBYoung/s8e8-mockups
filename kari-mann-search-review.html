Three numbers, and what they were really measuring

Each of these is a real thing someone saw. The question is what the tool behind it was counting.

What was seen
"We went from #4 to #23 on Google Maps"
What we found

A Maps position depends on where the phone is when the search happens. Google weights distance so heavily that the same search from the office, from a home in North Cape Coral, and from Fort Myers returns three different lists. A single check is a snapshot of one spot at one moment.

So we scanned a one mile grid of 65 points around the office for six different searches. Across all six, the listing appears at every point. For "dentist near me" and "local dentist" the average position is 4.4 and 4.5, and the practice is in the top 3 at more than 40% of the points. Nothing in any scan is near 23. Where the practice shows up on the map

How Google decides who shows up on the map. Google ranks map listings on three things: how well the listing matches what was typed, how far the person searching is from the practice, and how much Google trusts the business, which comes mostly from reviews and from how the practice is described across the web. Since a change in late 2021, distance carries the most weight of the three. The same search run from the office, from a home in North Cape Coral, and from Fort Myers returns three different lists. That is why one check on one phone can say #4 one day and #23 another, and why we measure the whole neighborhood at once instead.

The searches with "Cape Coral" in them are where the practice sits further back, and there is a specific reason. Two of the practices ahead of you, Smile Cape Coral and Cape Dental Care, are on the same stretch of Del Prado and have "Cape Coral" in their business name. Google gives a measurable lift to words in the listing name itself. That is allowed only when the words are the business's real, public-facing name. Adding them to a listing when they are not is against Google's rules and gets listings suspended, and we would not suggest renaming the practice for the sake of a map ranking either. What turns a listing into a patient is trust, and on that the practice leads: a 5.0 rating across 892 reviews, the strongest reputation of anyone in the top group. Visits from Google search: what changed and what did not

The chart shows clicks from Google search to the old website address and to the new one, with Google's three broad ranking updates marked. Clicks peaked in August 2025 and were sliding through the fall. They dropped further after the December update and again after the March update. By the time the new site went live on May 29, they were already at their low. The site launched into a decline that Google's changes had set in motion months earlier.

Who the readers were

The clicks that fell came from two articles, one on when to stop using gauze after an extraction and one on tooth sensitivity after a crown. Together they brought in more than 8 of every 10 search clicks, from readers all over the country. Their search visits started falling in January, months before the new site, when Google changed how it ranks health articles and began putting its own AI answer above the results for questions like these. Both articles are still live and still bring readers, just fewer.

About 1 in 1,000 of those blog readers ever called, booked, or sent a form, against 1 in 8 visitors to the practice pages. That is the same pattern on every practice we manage. Visits to the practice pages dropped in January too, and have held steady since the new site launched. Google found the new site and shows it as often as it showed the old one.

The site is three months old. The Cape Coral service pages went live on September 15 and 16, and the first pieces of your content calendar on September 18, with twenty-two more scheduled through next summer. New pages take months to earn their place on Google, which is why the plan runs continuously rather than as a one-time push.

Where the practice makes the biggest difference

Reviews are the one lever that sits entirely with the practice, and they are the second biggest factor in how Google ranks a listing after distance. Three things matter most.

Keep new reviews coming in every monthGoogle weighs recent reviews more than the lifetime total. A steady flow of new ones does more for the listing than the 892 already there, as strong as that number is.
Ask patients to write a sentence or two, not just starsA written review carries more weight than a rating alone. When a patient mentions the treatment they had and the part of Cape Coral or nearby they came from, Google can show that sentence right under the listing when someone searches for the same thing. That is what turns a listing into a click.
Reply to every review, promptlyResponses signal an active, attentive practice to Google and to the next patient reading. Short and personal is enough.
