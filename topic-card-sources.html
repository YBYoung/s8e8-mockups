Both sources agree. About 12,800 US searches a month, and 214 of our 237 practices were seen for 'dental implants'; 111 of them got real traffic from it. National searches eased over the year (19,000 to 12,800 a month) while our practices' impressions grew (128,000 to 241,000): practices are taking a bigger share of a slightly smaller pool.

Where these numbers come fromWhere these numbers come fromSearches: DataForSEO's clickstream estimate (anonymized browsing from a panel of real people, scaled to the whole US) for this exact phrase. Impressions: our own agency data, the Google Search Console impressions of every practice we manage, on searches containing the phrase, over the last 12 months. "Seen by" counts practices that showed up in Google for it at all; "real traffic" means 1,000+ impressions. The verdict calls a phrase sizable only when both sources show demand.

Why this matters The bars say how many people search; the teal line says whether dental offices like this one actually get seen for it, and whether that is rising or falling. When the two shapes part ways, that is the interesting case.
What has to be built The trend check already scans the corpus once per run for these phrases; it will also keep the monthly totals and practice counts from that same scan (no extra cost, no extra time) and store them on the run. The card reads them like every other number: precomputed, one request.
Other phrases The toggles switch the chart to 'tooth implant' (1,608 searches; 4,200 to 8,700 impressions across 63 to 96 practices) and 'implants teeth' (251 searches; 150 to 400 impressions across 11 to 26 practices). Real numbers from the same read.

B · One rule, two jobs The 10/14 rule says the calendar never recommends writing price-shopper pages (cheap, discounts, price lists, insurance, financing). Topic Cards borrowed Find gaps as-is, so the same rule also stopped the site check from asking insurance questions at all. The fix scopes the rule to what gets recommended, not to what gets checked.
C · Context for the question writer Today the writer gets only the topic name. "Pricing or cost transparency for any services" produced searches about SaaS pricing pages and attorneys. It will be told the topic is for a dental practice, and for Not-searched-by-name topics, to write how patients ask indirectly (in a chat, at the front desk, on a review).
