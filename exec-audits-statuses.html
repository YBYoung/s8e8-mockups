All counts are live production numbers verified 8/3/2026 (Asana portfolio + prod DB), except the per-category grades in Direction C, which are illustrative.

A

Separate the languages

Smallest change. The pace bar keeps its spot and its data but gets its own visual identity: a water-depth blue ramp (the deeper the blue, the deeper underwater) and its own band words — On pace / Slowing / Behind. Green, yellow, and red now appear on this page only for Asana client statuses. Copy is rewritten so each section says what it measures in its first line.

Where are we underwater · backlog pace by importance weightdeeper = further behind
1 category on pace · 9% of weight 2 slowing · 21% of weight 7 behind · 70% of weight
This grades our own delivery backlog per work category (report-card bands at 85 and 65), weighted by importance. It is about our pace, not client health — client health is the status card below.
Also in this direction: the status card's footer opens with "These are human-posted Asana statuses, not computed grades" · the audit tiles swap var(--blue)/var(--good) numbers for neutral ink so no audit number wears a status color · "will not agree" fine print replaced with one honest sentence. Bar segments get 2px gaps (both bars, matching mark spec).

Cost: frontend-only, ~4 files (ExecWeightBar, ExecHeadline copy, ExecClientStatusPanel/Strip copy, styles). No backend change. Shippable in one short session.

C

Answer "where" directly — grade ledger

Biggest change. The stacked bar never answers its own question — "where are we underwater" — without a trip to the evidence table. Replace it with a per-category ledger: every category as a dot on a 0–100 grade track with the 65/85 report-card cutoffs drawn in. Position carries the message; color collision disappears entirely. Includes B's reconciliation block on the status card.

Owner call on whether that consolidation is worth the churn.

Cost: frontend-only for the ledger (grades already in payload) + B's backend join for the status card. 2+ sessions including the consolidation questions.
